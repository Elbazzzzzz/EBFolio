Fight for Sight / Merger Digital Transformation

When two of the UK's leading eye health charities merged, they needed a digital presence that could serve blind and partially sighted people, their families, and the professionals who support them, all within one accessible site. I led UX across the full project, from discovery through to build handover, designing to WCAG 2.2 AA as a baseline and treating accessibility as the core design constraint, not a checklist item.

The Approach

Research

  • Stakeholder interviews to align both organisations on brand values, mission and vision before design started
  • Content audit across the Fight for Sight and Vision Foundation sites, exposing duplication and inconsistent structures
  • SEO and analytics audit to establish what traffic actually depended on
  • Personas covering people with sight loss using assistive technology, carers and family members, donors, and researchers
  • User research testing with 5 participants, including screen reader and magnification software users, run in both discovery and prototype phases
  • Competitor and sector benchmarking across charitable eye health organisations

Design

  • Sitemap and IA consolidating two content estates into a single structure
  • Content modelling to define repeating page structures and the component set
  • Functionality scoping through MoSCoW to keep the build realistic against timeline and budget
  • Design concept development from a moodboard direction agreed with the client
  • Components and page templates designed to WCAG 2.2 AA, including a persistent accessibility toolbar for colour mode and text size
  • 404 page design

Outputs

  • Full sitemap and IA documentation
  • Build specification covering browser support, hosting, and technical requirements
  • Accessibility-tested prototype handed to the development team for build
  • Stakeholder sign-off at each phase

The Brief

Fight for Sight and Vision Foundation merged as separate organisations with separate audiences, content estates, and brand identities, and needed a single digital presence for the newly merged charity. The Fight for Sight audience skewed toward researchers and donors, while the Vision Foundation audience skewed toward people with sight loss and their support networks. Neither existing site had been built with rigorous accessibility in mind.

The brief wasn't simply to merge content. It was to establish who the new site was actually for, and whether it could be designed so that a screen reader user and a first-time donor could both complete their goals without friction.

Testing with visually impaired users

I planned and ran the stakeholder interview programme in paired sessions, which kept the process manageable without losing depth, enabling us to get a wider breadth of user insights without stretching budgets or timelines. The interviews surfaced both recognised challenges and blind spots stakeholders hadn't considered the website could address, and running them this way built buy-in early. By the time we reached strategy, we had a coalition behind the direction rather than a room of competing agendas, and the playback confirmed the site could go further than the client had expected.

Merging two existing content architectures

A content audit across both existing sites showed how much duplication had built up and how inconsistently similar content had been structured. An SEO and analytics audit ran alongside it, so we could identify which pages were performing and which content could be removed.

From there, sitemap and IA work consolidated the two estates into one logical structure, and content modelling mapped the repeating structures across page types, which fed directly into the component set. Functionality scoping through MoSCoW kept the build realistic against what the timeline and budget could support.

404 page design showing desktop and mobile layouts: branded ‘sad eye’ mark, short reassurance copy, and a primary route back to the homepage.

Design decisions

WCAG 2.2 AA was the baseline throughout, checked at every stage rather than only at sign-off. This meant working alongside an existing brand guideline set, gently expanding these to meet the needs of the website and its users. Part of this involved some negotiation with a third-party branding agency on colour contrast, and considering astigmatism – which is a very common eye condition that contradicts the use of high contrast dark-mode styles.

The most significant decision was to build a persistent accessibility toolbar into the header, giving every visitor direct control over colour mode and text size rather than placing these controls in account settings or a separate page. Part of the user feedback included mention that many of Fight for Sight's users were newly diagnosed with conditions that would get harder to manage over time. This meant that it would not be helpful to assume that these users already knew where to find and how to use assistive technologies in the browser or on their device. This placement had a material effect on how consistently the controls were used.
