The Global Internet Forum to Counter Terrorism

Three interconnected research tools for digital counter-terrorism professionals: a searchable definitions framework, a thematic publication browser, and a risk mitigation resource. Built for policy makers, trust-and-safety teams, and legal experts across some of the world's largest platforms.

UX Lead

Sector

Global digital platform, counter-terrorism

Result

#1 most adopted GIFCT release

The global definitions research platform on desktop, used by policy makers and trust-and-safety teams to compare terrorism legislation across jurisdictions.

The brief

Global definitions of terrorism and violent extremism are fragmented, inconsistently worded, and buried in legislation across dozens of jurisdictions. Practitioners at major tech platforms needed to research and compare them quickly without becoming legal experts themselves. Alongside this, hundreds of academic publications needed to become browsable by topic, and a critical policy resource on terrorist designation lists needed to be navigable enough that people would actually read it in full, rather than skim it and move on.

Challenges

With roughly two months from brief to delivery, closing our knowledge gap in an unfamiliar domain was the first challenge. I recommended user interviews as the fastest route in. GIFCT connected us with policy makers from Airbnb, Twitter, and Discord for two 45 minute sessions, and desk research ran in parallel so we weren't starting from zero once the interviews began.

The client stakeholder group, spanning organisations including Facebook, YouTube, Discord, Zoom, Twitter, Tech Against Terrorism, and the Global Network on Extremism and Technology, brought close scrutiny and deep domain expertise. Findings had to hold up to expert review, not just inform an internal design direction.

The GNET tagging framework was the second challenge: seven broad themes, each with subtopics, some with a further layer beneath, and individual publications carrying between one and five applicable tags. Filtering the browser to match the internal structure of that taxonomy would have made sense on paper and failed in practice, since practitioners don't search the way a taxonomy is organised internally. A sitemap and scamps aligned all three tools, the definitions framework, the tagging browser, and the risk mitigation resource, into one coherent experience before any of it was built.

Design decisions

The definitions framework used filters by jurisdiction and governance type, reflecting the comparison tasks practitioners described in interviews. For the tagging browser, progressive disclosure managed the taxonomy's depth without overwhelming a first-time user. The risk mitigation resource needed a different treatment altogether: it's a policy document people need to read in full, not skim, so the structure was built to reward that kind of reading.

The design was built directly in code and tested with the original interview participants. Feedback led to wording adjustments that better matched the precise language used by legislation professionals, made ahead of the launch at GIFCT's global summit.

The research platform on mobile, with stacked navigation and browse controls.

Outcome

The platform became GIFCT's most adopted digital release. Practitioners reported significantly faster research across all three tools following launch.

The project's clearest external validation came afterward: it led to a direct recommendation from the International Centre for the Study of Radicalisation at King's College London, based on earlier work our agency had completed for them.
