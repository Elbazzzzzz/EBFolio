The Walk Wheel Cycle Trust / A Pan-Impairment Digital Map

Walk Wheel Cycle Trust

A research-led mapping tool for pan-impairment active travel, designed in partnership with Transport for All and co-designed with an embedded expert panel of disabled volunteers. Built around real barriers (gates, terrain, audio navigation gaps, surface quality) rather than assumed user fitness, with accessibility as a structural constraint not a feature layer.

The Approach

Research

  • Pan-impairment research in partnership with Transport for All and charity-sector partners
  • Embedded expert panel reviews with disabled volunteers, run continuously across the project
  • Stakeholder immersion sessions with internal Trust teams
  • Comparator analysis of five mapping tools (Google Maps, Apple Maps, OS Maps, Strava, Komoot) against accessibility-critical functionality

Design

  • Five pan-impairment personas with do-this and don't-do-this design guidance
  • User flows and content model covering the homepage, map launch, and barrier reporting journeys
  • Iterative design rounds, each reviewed by the expert panel and tested with real users.
  • Accessible interaction patterns built into the persistent UI

Outputs

  • Comparator analysis report
  • Persona set and design principles
  • High-fidelity Figma prototype, ready for volunteer testing
  • Findings report documenting research, rationale, and recommendations

The Problem

Mainstream mapping tools systematically fail disabled users. We confirmed this by auditing the five most-used tools on the market against accessibility-critical functionality, and each fell short in a different way. Wheelchair routing was inaccurate, offline access sat behind paywalls, audio navigation ignored hazards, and personalisation relied on exclusionary fitness language. The Walk Wheel Cycle Trust, a UK charity advocating for active travel, needed a mapping tool designed for the users these products overlook, without repeating the same mistakes.

Embedded Expert review

Pan-impairment design tends to fail at the seams. A pattern that works for one impairment can create friction for another, and those tensions only surface when lived experience is in the room continuously. So rather than recruiting a one-off cohort for end-stage testing, I worked with Transport for All and other charity-sector partners to build an embedded panel of disabled volunteers who reviewed designs with us throughout the project. Findings didn't sit in a discovery document; they kept shaping decisions through definition and design.

Personas built to surface conflict

I synthesised the research into five pan-impairment personas designed to expose competing needs rather than consensus. The audio navigation depth that supports a partially sighted runner creates friction for a commuter managing sensory overload, and the personas made tensions like this explicit so the team could test design decisions against them.

Wireframes to help to discuss and develop ideas alongside stakeholders and users.

A Barrier Map not a Routing Tool

Existing tools optimise for the fastest route and bury accessibility filters behind premium accounts or vague fitness language. We stepped away from suggesting routes altogether. The map surfaces community-reported barriers such as gates, gradients, path width and surface quality, and trusts users to judge whether a route works for their needs. To keep that data reliable without centralised audits, every barrier shows its last-reported date, users confirm or clear barriers when they select them, and new points only appear once reported by more than one user. Accessibility controls for text size and contrast sit in the persistent UI rather than in a settings menu.

Outcome and Legacy

The work unlocked funding for the next phase. The Figma prototype was built to a fidelity the Trust could take directly into testing with its NCN volunteer network, hundreds of community members already engaged with active travel on the ground, so research could continue without waiting on us. A findings report documenting the research, comparator analysis, personas and design rationale means the thinking outlasts the engagement, and the embedded panel model is a methodology the Trust can carry into future projects.
